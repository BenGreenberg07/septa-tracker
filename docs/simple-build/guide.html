Build Guide

Revision 2: a light box made from the 1/16 in. aluminum you already have, cut and scored on the CNC, folded by hand, riveted and sealed, with printed vents, a commercial TV mount, and your existing plastic sheet as the window.

Before you drill the stationThe building belongs to SEPTA, so mounting needs their written OK first, and they may want their own crew to do the drilling or the mains connection. The outlet is an electrician's job. Everything else in this guide is a normal workshop job.

How it works

  • Box: four walls cut from 1/16 in. sheet and folded into channels, with a 1 in. lip along each long edge. The folds make thin sheet stiff. A flat back panel is riveted to the back lips and sealed, and stays on for good.
  • CNC: every rivet pilot, vent window and fold line is cut in one setup, so the parts line up without measuring.
  • Window: your existing plastic sheet sits on foam on the front lips and is held by a clamp frame, screwed into rubber well nuts. Removing those screws is how you open the case.
  • Inside: the LED panels bolt to two rails across the top and bottom. The PSU and Pi screw to the back.
  • Air: printed louvres in the bottom and top walls, with mesh on the inside, let warm air rise straight through.
  • Wall: a commercial outdoor TV mount. Its plate goes on the brick, its arms bolt to the case, and the arms hook on and lock.

Three decisions, and why

Vents: yes, mesh on the inside

Put the printed louvres outside and the mesh behind them. The slats keep falling water off the mesh, and being inside keeps it out of the weather.

Freezing is not a danger to the board. Vents can only ice up when it is below freezing outside, which is exactly when the board needs the least cooling. If one ices over, the box just runs a little warmer until it melts. The dangerous case is a blocked vent in July, so clean the mesh each spring.

  • Use ordinary aluminum insect screen (about 1.5 mm openings), not fine mesh. Fine mesh holds a film of water that freezes.
  • Print in ASA. PETG is fine in the shade. Never PLA: it softens around 55 °C and cracks in the cold.
  • Screw the vents in so they come out for cleaning.
  • Drill four 1/8 in. weep holes in the bottom wall, because the lips make it a shallow tray.

Humidity: worth caring about, and easy to manage

Damp air does little harm on its own. The problem is liquid water, which forms when a surface is colder than the air's dew point.

Do

  • Leave it powered, all year. The board's own heat keeps surfaces above the dew point. Overnight dimming is fine.
  • Keep the vents open. A sealed box traps moisture every time it warms and cools. In a field test by Gore, a vent maker, a sealed box with desiccant had serious condensation by day 4, and the vented box shed moisture much better.
  • Fit drip shields over the PSU and Pi, bent from scrap, so a drop from the top wall lands on metal.
  • Run every cable in from below, with a drip loop.
  • Conformal-coat the Pi and bonnet (step 12) as a backstop.

Do not

  • Seal the vents for winter. It makes condensation worse.
  • Add desiccant. In a vented box it saturates in days.
  • Cold-start after days unplugged on a damp morning. Open the frame and let it dry first.
  • Coat connectors, the SD slot, the USB ports, the fan or the header pins. Mask them first.
Heat and coldExpect the inside to run about 5 to 10 °C above outside. In a 38 °C heat wave, that is the top of the Pi's 50 °C rating, so keep it out of direct sun and keep the Active Cooler.

The build

Sixteen steps. In the pictures, orange is what that step adds. Label every part with masking tape as it comes off the machine.

partstools
1
Test the alloy and the score first
45 min
scrap of your sheetCNC, V-bitcalipersclamp, bar
  1. Cut three 6 × 4 in. coupons and score each across the middle, at the three depths shown. Take two passes of about 0.015 in. each.
  2. Fold each by hand to 90° over a clamped bar, once, score on the inside.
  3. Look at the outside of each corner under a bright light. Use the deepest score that folds cleanly and has no cracks.
  4. Rivet a scrap to the fold of the best coupon and try to break it by hand, so you know what a good one feels like.
If every coupon cracksThe sheet may be 6061-T6 and not 5052 (5051 is an unusual label, so check). Stop scoring. Use a brake with a 1/8 in. inside radius, with a shallow 0.005 in. score only as a marker. The rest of this guide still applies.
2
Draw the flat parts and lay them out
2 h
your measurementsCAD/CAM software
  1. Draw each flat part from the table, on separate layers: holes, scores, windows, outlines.
  2. Rivet pilots: 1/8 in. plunges, centred 15/32 in. from the edge on every back lip and around the matching edge of the back, every 4 in.; 3 per leg on the corner pieces.
  3. Windows: four 8 × 2-1/2 in. in each of the top and bottom walls, positions in step 10's drawing, plus the small vent-screw pilots around them.
  4. Back panel: four 5/16 in. circles for the VESA bolts (600 × 400 mm, centred), and pilots for the PSU, Pi and earth bolt.
  5. Bottom wall: four 1/8 in. weep holes and a 13/16 in. gland circle in the gap between windows 3 and 4.
  6. Tell the CAM to cut on the inside face: the scores must land inside the fold.
3
Cut on the CNC
2 to 3 h
sheetspoilboard, screwsO-flute bitV-bitWD-40
  1. Empty the dust collector first. Aluminum chips in wood dust are a fire risk. Wear eye protection.
  2. Surface the spoilboard, then screw the sheet down through waste areas, every 6 to 8 in. Thin sheet lifts, and lifted sheet ruins scores.
  3. Cut in the order shown: holes, scores, windows, outlines. Outlines come last so the sheet stays held down until the end.
  4. Starting point for the 1/8 in. O-flute: 18,000 RPM, 30 in./min, 0.020 in. per pass, three or four passes, with WD-40 on the cut. Listen and adjust: gummy chips mean too slow a feed.
  5. Leave small tabs on outlines. Knock parts free, then file tabs flush and deburr every edge. Sharp aluminum cuts hands and wires.
Vacuum tablesDo not spray lubricant on a vacuum table: it pulls through to the pump.
4
Fold the walls, frame and corners
90 min
cut partssteel angle or hardwood barC-clampssquare
  1. Lay a wall on the bench, score up, with the score on the edge of the bench bar. Clamp a second bar over it every 8 in.
  2. Push the lip up to 90° in one slow, even motion, using a straight board across the whole length so it does not kink.
  3. Fold the two lips on each wall, all the same way, so the wall is a C. Check each wall is straight.
  4. Fold the clamp frame pieces and the four corner pieces. Bend the corner pieces in a vise without scoring: they carry load.
How strong is a scored fold?Only as strong as the metal left under the groove. That is fine here: the lips are held flat by the riveted back and foam, and the back panel carries the load that would rack the box.
5
Cut the thick bars
30 min
2 sticks of 1/8 in. anglehacksaw or mitre sawfile
  1. Cut the two rails (W − 1/2) and the two backing bars (28 in.). Every end is square.
  2. File each cut end until it no longer catches a fingernail, and label each bar.
6
Learn the sealed rivet
15 min
2 scraps3 rivetssiliconerivet tooldrill, #20 bit
  1. Clamp the two parts together. Open the 1/8 in. pilot to #20 (0.161 in.) through both at once, so the holes line up perfectly.
  2. Unclamp, run a thin bead of silicone between the parts, and clamp again.
  3. Push a rivet in from the outside, flange flat, and squeeze until the pin snaps. Do three on scrap first.
SpacingEvery 4 in. on long joints. If a hole misses, drill a fresh one beside it and rivet both. Wipe the squeeze-out before it skins.
7
Build the rim
60 min
4 walls4 corner pieces24 rivetssiliconerivet toolsquareclamps
  1. Stand the four walls on edge, lips facing inward. The sides fit between the top and bottom walls.
  2. Put a corner piece inside each corner (1 to 4) with silicone on both faces, and clamp.
  3. Check each corner is square, then drill and rivet: 3 rivets into each wall at each corner.
  4. Measure both diagonals. If they differ by more than 1/8 in., rack the box square before the last rivet goes in.
8
Rivet in the panel rails
40 min
2 rails12 rivetsthe panel arrayrivet toolclamps
  1. Dry-fit first. Set the array inside and find where its backplate brackets can bolt to a rail across the top and one across the bottom. The LED faces should end up about 1/4 in. behind the front lips.
  2. Rivet each rail to the inside of the top or bottom wall: flat leg against the wall, running back from 1-1/4 in. behind the front edge, the other leg standing to take the backplate bolts. Six rivets per rail.
  3. Keep the rails clear of the vent windows and of the well-nut zone in the front 1 in. of each wall.
  4. Drill the rails for the backplate bolts now, while you can still reach.
9
Prepare the back panel
60 min
back2 backing bars4 M8 bolts, lock nuts, washersPSUdrill
  1. Clamp the two backing bars inside the back, one on the top pair of VESA holes and one on the bottom pair. Drill through the bars from the panel's holes.
  2. Bolt on each mount arm from outside with an M8 bolt, washers, and a lock nut inside. Silicone under each washer. Check the arms hang the right way up.
  3. Fix the PSU with M4 screws through the back, with a dab of silicone under each head.
  4. Fix the Pi's nylon standoffs, low and left, near the cool intake.
  5. Fit the earth bolt: #10 stainless screw through bare metal with a star washer. Scrape any coating off first.
Which way is upHold the back as it will hang before you drill anything. Mount arms have a top and a bottom.
10
Print and fit the vents, fit the gland
30 min, plus printing
8 printed vents, 8 framesinsect screensiliconecable glandscrewdriverscissors
  1. Print eight vents in ASA: at least 4 perimeters, 30 % infill, with an enclosure so the part does not warp. Allow for the 1/16 in. radius the CNC leaves in window corners.
  2. Cut eight patches of screen 1/2 in. larger than each window, and trap each between its vent and its inner frame.
  3. Run a thin bead of silicone under each vent's flange and screw it in from outside with stainless screws. On the top wall, tilt the slats so openings face front. On the bottom wall, let them face down.
  4. Fit the cable gland in the bottom wall, nut inside.
Why this much ventThe board normally gives off about 50 W of heat. Each wall has four 20 sq in. windows. Louvres and mesh take roughly two thirds of that, which still leaves enough opening for warm air to rise out on its own.
11
Fit the back
60 min
rimback panelabout 34 rivetssiliconerivet tool
  1. Clamp the back onto the rim and open the pilots to #20 through both. Unclamp and clean out chips.
  2. Run a continuous bead of silicone along all four back lips.
  3. Press the back on and rivet every 4 in., working out from the middle of each side. Wipe the squeeze-out.
12
Electronics, coating and drip shields
90 min
Pi + bonnetstandoffsearth wire16 AWG red/blackconformal coating2 drip shieldsscrewdriverswire strippers
  1. Coat the Pi and bonnet off the board. Mask every connector, the SD slot, USB and Ethernet ports, GPIO pins and the fan. Two light coats, and let them cure fully (see the can) before powering on.
  2. Fit the Pi with its Active Cooler, riser and 16 mm standoffs, then the bonnet.
  3. Fold and screw the two scrap drip shields above the PSU and Pi, tilted so water runs off to the side, with a gap so air still reaches them.
  4. With the cord unplugged, run it in through the gland with a drip loop outside, and tighten the gland.
  5. Connect green earth wire from the PSU's earth terminal to the earth bolt.
  6. Give the Pi its own 16 AWG pair straight from the PSU terminals (sheet 4 of the wiring document).
MainsOnly the cord passes through the gland; the PSU's mains connections stay as they are. Anything on the 120 V side is an electrician's job.
13
Panels in, first light
45 min
panel arraybackplate boltswrench
  1. Lower the panel array in through the front and bolt its backplates to both rails. Route ribbons and power away from sharp edges.
  2. Plug in and run the board. Check every panel lights, the right way up, before you close anything.
  3. Leave it on for 20 minutes and look for flicker on a white screen (the power test in HARDWARE.md).
14
Window and clamp frame
60 min
plastic sheetfoam tapeclamp frame (4 pieces)12 well nuts12 security screwsdrill, 3/16 + 3/8 in.T-25 pin bit
  1. Stick foam tape all round the front lips (1). Clean the plastic sheet and peel any film.
  2. Lay the sheet on the tape (2). Stick foam tape inside each clamp-frame piece's front leg.
  3. Set the clamp frame on (3): top and bottom pieces full width, side pieces between them. Clamp it.
  4. Drill 3/16 in. through the frame's side leg and the wall, about 5/8 in. back from the front edge: 4 holes along the top, 4 along the bottom, 2 down each side.
  5. Take the frame off. Open just the wall holes to 3/8 in. and push a well nut into each from outside. Refit the frame and drive the screws until the foam squeezes about halfway. No tighter: acrylic cracks if clamped hard.
No holes in the plasticNothing goes through the sheet. It floats between two strips of foam, so it can grow and shrink with the seasons without cracking.

  2. Smooth it with a wet finger, pull the tape, and let it cure overnight.

Hanging it

Before you drillSEPTA's OK in writing; two people; two ladders or a platform. Drill one test hole low and out of sight first. If the dust comes out red and the hole stays crisp, the brick is solid and sleeve anchors are fine. If it is crumbly or breaks into a hollow, stop: use the epoxy method in docs/mounting-and-case-plan.pdf instead.
A
Mark the wall
20 min
wall platelevelpainter's tapetape measure
  1. Tape the case outline on the wall, bottom edge 7 to 8 ft above the platform.
  2. Hold the plate centred inside the outline and level. Mark 6 slots, each landing in the middle of a whole brick (or in mortar joints, if SEPTA prefers).
B
Anchors and the wall plate
45 min
6 sleeve anchorsfender washersdrill, 1/4 in. masonry bithammerwrench
  1. Start each hole with the drill on rotary (hammer off) so the brick face does not chip, and go 1/2 in. deeper than the anchor.
  2. Blow the dust out. Tap each anchor in through the plate.
  3. Fender washer under each nut. Tighten until snug, then one more turn, and check the plate is still level.
C
Hang it and lock it
30 min
the casepadlock or security screw2 people
  1. Two people lift the case until its arms are just above the plate, then lower it until they hook on.
  2. Level it with the mount's adjustment screws if it has them.
  3. Rotate the mount's locking tabs and fit a padlock or security screw, so it cannot be lifted off.
  4. Plug into the GFCI outlet (fitted by the electrician) with a drip loop, and press TEST, then RESET.
The cheaper route: a Z-barSave about $160 by using a 30 in. Hangman HM-30D Z-bar instead of a TV mount. Bolt the case half high on the back through one 32 in. 1/8 in. backing bar, hook pointing down. Fix the wall half to the brick with 4 anchors, hook pointing up. Then add two 2 in. long pieces of 1-1/2 in. angle at the bottom, one leg anchored to the wall and one under the case with a security screw, so it cannot be lifted off.

Looking after it

WhenDo this
You need to get insideUndo the 12 clamp-frame screws (T-25 pin bit) and lift off the frame and sheet. Everything is reachable from the front once the panel array is unbolted.
Water inside after rainLook for a missed silicone seam at the back corners, then for flattened foam at the window. Re-seal; replace foam that has taken a set.
Droplets insideCondensation, not a leak. Check the vents are clear and the board has stayed powered. Check the weep holes are open.
It runs hot in summerClean the mesh. If it is still hot, the full plan adds a small fan at the top vent.
Window scratched or cloudyMild soap and water only. No glass cleaner: ammonia ruins acrylic and polycarbonate.
Taking it downTwo people: unlock the mount's tabs, lift up, pull away.

Every spring

  • Clean the window and the vent mesh; check the weep holes
  • Look over the silicone and the foam; re-seal anything cracked
  • Check the anchors and the mount locks are tight
  • Press TEST and RESET on the GFCI outlet
