Simple Case
Build Guide

The quickest, cheapest way to get the board weatherproofed and on the wall: an aluminium-panel box you can build with a drill, a utility knife and a rivet tool, using the plastic sheet you already have as the window.

Before you drill the stationThe building belongs to SEPTA, so mounting needs their written OK first, and they may want their own crew to do the drilling or the mains connection. The outlet is an electrician's job. Everything else in this guide is a normal workshop job.

How it works

The case is a shallow box made of aluminium composite panel (ACM): two thin aluminium skins with a plastic core, the material most outdoor signs are made of. It cuts with a utility knife and holds rivets well.

  • Box: four ACM strips form the rim, joined at the corners with short pieces of aluminium angle and pop rivets. An ACM back is riveted on and sealed, and stays on for good.
  • Window: your existing plastic sheet sits on foam tape and is held by a clamp frame of aluminium angle, screwed into rubber well nuts. Removing those screws is how you open the case.
  • Inside: the LED panels bolt to two rails across the top and bottom. The PSU and Pi screw to the back.
  • Air: vent windows in the bottom and top let warm air rise straight through. The station canopy keeps rain off, so there is no hood.
  • Wall: a 30 in. aluminium Z-bar cleat (one half on the wall, one on the case) carries the weight. Two small anti-lift brackets at the bottom stop anyone lifting it off.

What it leaves out, on purpose

No custom metalwork, no epoxy anchors, no powder coating, no fan, no rain hood (it hangs under the canopy). If it ever moves somewhere exposed, screw a strip of aluminium drip edge over the top vents on 1/2 in. spacers. If it runs hot in summer, or the brick turns out to be soft, the full plan (docs/mounting-and-case-plan.pdf) has the heavier-duty version of each part.

The build

Fifteen steps. In every picture, orange is what that step adds. Work on a big table with a blanket on it so the ACM faces do not scratch.

partstools
Two habits that save the whole buildLabel every part with masking tape as you cut it. And dry-fit (hold it together with clamps) before you drill anything you cannot undo.
1
Mark the ACM
20 min
2 ACM sheetstape measuresquaremarker
  1. Leave the protective film on, and mark on the film.
  2. Sheet 1: mark the back, W × H, in one corner.
  3. Sheet 2: mark four strips 4-1/2 in. wide along the 48 in. length.
  4. Pick which face goes outside (black looks best around a screen) and write "OUT" on every piece.
2
Cut the ACM: score and snap
45 min
marked ACMutility knife, fresh bladesmetal straightedge2 clampsfile
  1. Clamp the straightedge on the line. Score 4 to 5 light passes, until you are through the top skin into the black core.
  2. Slide the sheet so the line sits on the table edge, scored side up. Press the overhang down firmly: it cracks along the line.
  3. Cut the bottom skin in the crease with the knife, then file the edges smooth.
  4. Cut the strips to length: top and bottom W, sides H − 1/4 in.
TipPractise once on the spare. Many light passes beat one heavy pass, which wanders off the line.
3
Cut the aluminium angle
45 min
4 thin + 2 thick angle stickshacksaw or mitre sawfilemasking tape
  1. Cut the pieces shown. Every end is square: there are no mitres anywhere in this build.
  2. File each cut end until it no longer catches a fingernail. Sharp aluminium cuts hands and wires.
  3. Label each piece: "back top", "rail top", and so on.
4
Cut the vent windows
40 min
top and bottom stripsdrill, 1/2 in. bitjigsaw, metal bladefile
  1. On the top and bottom strips, mark two 16 × 1-3/4 in. windows, 4 in. in from each end, starting 2-1/8 in. back from the front edge.
  2. Drill a 1/2 in. hole inside each corner (red circles).
  3. Join the holes with the jigsaw, supporting the strip on both sides of the cut so it does not chatter. File smooth.
Why these sizesThe board gives off about 80 W of heat. Two windows per strip make about 56 sq in. of opening at top and bottom, enough for warm air to rise out on its own even with screen behind it.
5
Learn the pop rivet
10 min
scrap ACM and angle3 rivetsrivet tooldrill, 3/16 in. bit
  1. Clamp the two parts together and drill 3/16 in. through both at once, so the holes line up.
  2. Push a rivet in from the side you can reach, flange flat on the part.
  3. Put the tool on the pin and squeeze until it snaps. Do three on scrap before the real thing.
SpacingEvery 6 in. along long joints; 2 per leg on short pieces. If a hole misses, drill a fresh one beside it and rivet both.
6
Build the rim
45 min
4 ACM strips4 corner angles16 rivetsrivet toolsquareclamps
  1. Stand the four strips on edge, "OUT" faces outward. The sides fit between the top and bottom strips.
  2. Put a 3 in. corner angle inside each corner (1 to 4), centred in the depth, and clamp.
  3. Check the corner is square, then drill and rivet: 2 rivets into each strip at each corner.
  4. Measure both diagonals. If they differ by more than 1/8 in., push the long diagonal in and re-rivet that corner.
7
Rivet in the panel rails
40 min
2 thick rails12 rivetsthe panel arrayrivet toolclamps
  1. Dry-fit first. Lay the rim face-down, set the panel array inside, and find where its backplate brackets can bolt to a rail across the top and one across the bottom. Mark the rail depth so the LED faces end up about 1/4 in. behind the front edge.
  2. Rivet each rail along the inside of the top or bottom strip: one leg flat on the strip, the other standing up to take the backplate bolts. Six rivets per rail.
  3. Keep the rails in front of the vent windows, not over them.
  4. Drill the rails for the backplate bolts now, while you can still reach.
8
Prepare the back panel
60 min
back panelbacking barZ-bar, case half#10-32 screws, lock nutsnylon washersPSUdrill
  1. Cleat: on the outside, centre the case half of the Z-bar near the top, hook pointing down (towards the floor once hanging). Put the 32 in. backing bar behind it on the inside. Drill through all three and fit 5 bolts: nylon washers under the heads, lock nuts inside.
  2. PSU: hold the PSU where step 7's picture shows it, mark its 4 mounting holes, drill, and fix it with M4 screws from outside, a dab of silicone under each head.
  3. Pi: drill 4 holes for the nylon standoffs where you can reach them from the front.
Hook directionHold the back up the way it will hang before you drill. The case half hooks down, the wall half hooks up. Getting it backwards is the classic mistake.
9
Screen the vents, fit the cable gland
30 min
insect screensiliconecable glanddrill, 13/16 in. (or a step bit)scissors
  1. Cut four screen patches 1 in. bigger than each vent window all round.
  2. Run silicone around each window on the inside, press the screen into it, and let it set.
  3. Drill a 13/16 in. hole in the bottom strip below the PSU and fit the cable gland, nut on the inside.
10
Fit the back
60 min
rimback panel4 back-edge anglesabout 40 rivetssiliconeearth ring terminalrivet tool
  1. Rivet the four back-edge angles inside the rim, flush with its back edge: one leg on the strip, one leg facing back.
  2. Run a bead of silicone along the back-facing legs.
  3. Press the back panel on (cleat outside, hook down). Rivet through the back into the angles every 6 in. Wipe off the squeeze-out.
  4. Put the earth ring terminal under one of these rivets, near the PSU, on bare metal: scrape the paint off at that spot first.
11
Electronics and wiring
60 min
Pi + bonnetstandoffsearth wire16 AWG red/blackscrewdriverswire strippers
  1. Fit the Pi on its standoffs.
  2. With the cord unplugged, run it in through the gland with a drip loop outside, and tighten the gland.
  3. Connect the green earth wire from the PSU's earth terminal to the ring terminal from step 10.
  4. While you are in here, give the Pi its own 16 AWG pair straight from the PSU terminals: the under-voltage fix in HARDWARE.md.
MainsOnly the cord passes through the gland; the PSU's mains connections stay as they are. Anything on the 120 V side is an electrician's job.
12
Panels in, first light
45 min
panel arraybackplate boltswrench
  1. Lower the panel array in and bolt its backplates to both rails. Route ribbons and power so nothing rests on a sharp edge.
  2. Plug in and run the board. Check every panel lights, the right way up, before you close anything.
  3. Leave it on for 20 minutes and look for flicker on a white screen (the power test in HARDWARE.md).
13
Window and clamp frame
60 min
plastic sheetfoam tapeclamp frame (4 angles)12 well nuts12 security screwsdrill, 3/16 + 3/8 in.T-25 pin bit
  1. Stick foam tape all round the rim's front edge (1). Clean the plastic sheet and peel any film.
  2. Lay the sheet on the tape (2). Stick foam tape inside each clamp-frame piece's front leg.
  3. Set the clamp frame on (3): top and bottom pieces full width, side pieces between them. Clamp it.
  4. Drill 3/16 in. through the frame's side leg and the rim: 4 holes along the top, 4 along the bottom, 2 down each side.
  5. Take the frame off. Open just the rim holes to 3/8 in. and push a well nut into each from outside. Refit the frame and drive the screws until the foam squeezes about halfway. No tighter: acrylic cracks if it is clamped hard.
No holes in the plasticNothing goes through the sheet. It floats between two strips of foam, so it can grow and shrink with the seasons without cracking.
14
Seal it
30 min, then overnight
siliconecaulk gunmasking tape, rag
  1. Mask both sides of each outside seam. Run a thin bead of silicone where the back meets the rim, and down each corner.
  2. Smooth it with a wet finger, pull the tape, and let it cure overnight.
  3. Do not silicone the clamp frame. Its foam tape seals it, and it has to come off for service.

3 h, mostly waiting

Hanging it

Before you drillSEPTA's OK in writing; two people; two ladders or a platform. Drill one test hole low and out of sight first. If the dust comes out red and the hole stays crisp, the brick is solid and sleeve anchors are fine. If it is crumbly or breaks into a hollow, stop: use the epoxy method in the full plan instead.
A
Mark the wall
20 min
wall half of the Z-barlevelpainter's tapetape measure
  1. Tape the case outline on the wall, bottom edge 7 to 8 ft above the platform.
  2. On the case, measure from its top edge down to the cleat. Draw a level line that far below the top of the outline.
  3. Hold the wall half on the line, hook pointing up, centred. Mark 4 holes, each in the middle of a whole brick (or in mortar joints, if SEPTA prefers).
B
Anchors and the wall cleat
30 min
4 sleeve anchorsnylon washersdrill, 1/4 in. masonry bithammerwrench
  1. Start each hole with the drill on rotary (hammer off) so the brick face does not chip, and go 1/2 in. deeper than the anchor.
  2. Blow the dust out. Tap each anchor in through the cleat (open the cleat's holes with a drill if the anchors don't pass).
  3. Nylon washer under each nut; tighten until snug, then one more turn. Check the cleat is still level.
C
Hang it and lock it
30 min
the case2 anti-lift brackets2 sleeve anchors2 well nuts, 2 security screws2 people
  1. Two people lift the case until its cleat is just above the wall cleat, then lower it straight down. It drops about 1/2 in. as the hooks engage.
  2. Hold each anti-lift bracket in place (one leg on the wall, one under the case), mark, then drill the wall for a sleeve anchor and the case bottom for a well nut.
  3. Fit the anchors, then the security screws. Now it cannot be lifted off or pulled out at the bottom.
  4. Plug into the GFCI outlet (fitted by the electrician) with a drip loop, and press TEST, then RESET.

Looking after it

WhenDo this
You need to get insideUndo the 12 clamp-frame screws (T-25 pin bit) and lift off the frame and sheet. Everything is reachable from the front once the panel array is unbolted.
Water inside after rainLook for a missed silicone seam at the back corners, then for flattened foam at the window. Re-seal; replace foam that has taken a set.
It runs hot in summerCheck the vent screens are clear. If it is still hot, the full plan adds a small fan at the top vent.
Window scratched or cloudyMild soap and water only. No glass cleaner: ammonia ruins acrylic and polycarbonate. Replace with UV polycarbonate if it has yellowed.
Taking it downTwo people: undo the two anti-lift screws, lift up 1/2 in., pull away.

Every spring

  • Clean the window and the vent screens
  • Look over the silicone and the foam; re-seal anything cracked
  • Check the anchors and anti-lift screws are tight
  • Press TEST and RESET on the GFCI outlet
