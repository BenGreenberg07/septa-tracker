Before anything is drilled or ordered This is a student design plan, not a stamped engineering drawing. The station belongs to SEPTA, so mounting on the building needs SEPTA's written approval, and they may require their own engineer or contractor to review the anchors and do the installation. The load numbers in section 3 are estimates to size parts and start that conversation. Mains wiring at the station must be done by a licensed electrician or SEPTA facilities.

1. Summary

The display (16 LED panels, 1,024 × 512 mm active area, Raspberry Pi 5, 5 V / 100 A supply) moves from its current plastic case with a slide-in back into a powder-coated aluminium case, hung on a brick wall at the station with an aluminium Z-clip cleat and stainless epoxy anchors.

3. Mounting

3.1 Why a Z-clip cleat

  • The case hangs by gravity on a full-width rail, so the load spreads along the wall instead of sitting on a few points.
  • It self-levels and self-centres while two people lower it on, then lifts straight off for bench work.
  • The sign-industry version (aluminium Z-clips) is cheaper and better documented than a custom French cleat. Monarch recommends its MFSTR-050 high-load profile where larger bolts and masonry are involved: 1/2 in. projection, 7/16 in. lift-off.
  • Monarch states that capacity is set by the fasteners and the wall, not the clip, and that in lab tests the fasteners failed before the clips deformed. The anchors are therefore the part to get right.
Lift-off clearanceLowering the case onto the rail needs at least 7/16 in. of clear space above the hood to the canopy or any overhang. Plan for 2 in. so it can be handled comfortably.

3.2 Anchors into brick

ItemSpecification
Anchor3/8 in.-16 type 316 stainless threaded rod set in Simpson SET-3G epoxy (two-part, rated for concrete and masonry)
Quantity6: four through the wall rail, one at each of the two bottom brackets. One anchor type and one drill bit for the whole job.
Solid brick7/16 in. carbide bit, rotary only (hammer off). Embed at least 3-1/2 in., more if the manufacturer's brick table requires it.
Hollow-cored brick9/16 in. hole with a Simpson Opti-Mesh 3GWS373P screen tube (3/8 in. rod, 3-1/2 in. long), so the epoxy keys behind the shell instead of running into the void.
Hole cleaningBlow out, brush with a Simpson ETB nylon brush sized to the hole, blow out again. A dusty hole is the most common cause of a weak adhesive anchor.
CureSET-3G cures slowly and installs only at 40 °F or warmer. Follow the cure-time table on the cartridge and hang the case the next day or later.
PlacementCentre anchors in whole bricks or in mortar joints as SEPTA prefers (below). Stay clear of wall edges, openings and the top course, and follow the manufacturer's edge and spacing distances. Slot the rail holes so anchors can land where the brick is sound.
Alternative adhesiveHilti HIT-HY 270 has an ICC-ES evaluation (ESR-4144) that specifically covers unreinforced and multi-wythe brick. If SEPTA's engineer wants published design values for historic brick, use it instead: same geometry, with Hilti rods and dispenser.
Fallback3/8 in. stainless (18-8) sleeve anchors, if adhesive is not allowed. Do not use wedge anchors in old brick: they can split it.
Brick or mortar joint?Whole bricks are stronger. Mortar joints are the usual choice on historic buildings because they can be raked out and repointed later, leaving no damage to the brick face. This is SEPTA's decision. First, test-drill one hole in an inconspicuous spot to learn whether the bricks are solid or cored and how sound the mortar is.

Requirement: each anchor's allowable load in this brick (from the manufacturer's masonry tables or ESR, not the concrete tables) must be at least 150 lb in tension and 100 lb in shear. 3/8 in. adhesive anchors in sound brick are normally well above this, but this is the number to confirm. The 30 psf wind figure is a deliberately conservative placeholder; under a canopy the real value is much lower. SEPTA's engineer should set it.

3.4 Corrosion and theft

  • Stainless only for anything in the wall or outside the case: plated steel rusts and stains brick.
  • Isolate stainless from aluminium with nylon shoulder (sleeve) washers under every nut on the rails and brackets, plus anti-seize or a corrosion-inhibiting compound on threads. Without isolation the aluminium corrodes around each bolt in wet weather.
  • Anti-lift: the bottom brackets are fixed to the case with pin-in Torx screws into rivnuts. Without them, a Z-clip mount lifts off as easily for a thief as for a maintainer.

3.5 Installation sequence

  1. SEPTA approval; location and outlet agreed; test hole drilled; anchor type and placement settled.
  2. Hold the wall rail level at its final height (bottom of the case 2.1 to 2.4 m above the platform) and mark anchor centres on sound brick or joints.
  3. Drill rotary-only; clean (blow, brush, blow); insert screen tubes if needed; inject epoxy from the bottom of the hole up; twist the rods in.
  4. Let it cure fully. Fit the rail with nylon washers and stainless nuts, and check it is level.
  5. Locate the two bottom-bracket anchors from the case itself (or a template taken from it) and set them the same way.
  6. Two people, on two ladders or a platform, lift the case above the rail and lower it until the clips engage. Never one person.
  7. Fit the anti-lift screws at the bottom brackets. Plug in with a drip loop and test the GFCI.

4. Case

4.1 Structure

  • Frame: two rectangular rings of 1 × 1 × 1/8 in. 6061-T6 angle (front and back), joined by short depth members at the corners and at the centre line, bolted through laser-cut gusset plates, about 9 m (30 ft) of angle in total. The frame carries every load: the case rail, the brackets, the panel backplates and the electronics tray all bolt to it, never to a bare skin.
  • Skins: 0.080 in. 5052-H32 aluminium, laser-cut and bent with 15 mm return flanges, powder-coated. The front bezel, back, top and bottom are each split into two halves at the centre line, where the frame has a member, so every part fits the limits in Figure 3.
  • Panels: the existing four aluminium backplates with their 16 panels bolt to the front ring, keeping the current wiring and serpentine layout. Leave 3 to 5 mm between the LED faces and the window.
  • Electronics: the PSU and Pi sit on a removable 2 mm aluminium tray bolted to the back ring's cross-member, so the back halves come off without unplugging anything. The PSU's metal body sits flat on the tray to spread its heat.

4.2 Fabrication

  • Order cut, bent and powder-coated parts from SendCutSend (5052 aluminium), and check their live size limits and powder-coating rules (each part needs a small hanging hole) when quoting. OSH Cut is a comparable alternative.
  • Colour: matte black or dark grey around the window improves contrast. Under a canopy the extra solar heat of a dark case matters little; in full sun prefer a lighter grey.
  • Mask the grounding points (one boss on each skin and on the frame) from powder coat, because powder coat insulates.

4.3 Closures and fasteners

  • The back halves and service hatch close with #10-32 pin-in Torx (T-25) stainless button-head screws into 10-32 rivnuts about every 150 mm, which compresses the gasket evenly. Aluminium rivnuts avoid a stainless-aluminium couple in the sheet; if stainless rivnuts are used, add anti-seize.
  • Rivnuts are set with an Astro 1442 hand rivnut tool plus its separate 10-32 mandrel (the kit's own sizes do not include 10-32).
  • Every skin-to-frame seam gets closed-cell EPDM foam tape, 1/8 × 1/2 in., compressed by the screws.
  • A service hatch (about 200 × 100 mm) in the bottom face under the Pi gives access to its network and USB ports without taking the case down.

4.4 Window

  • Material: UV-protected polycarbonate, 3/16 in. (4.5 mm), light smoke grey, cut to about 1,075 × 560 mm by TAP Plastics. For more scratch and graffiti resistance, use their hard-coated (AR) polycarbonate, which is also UV-enhanced.
  • Not acrylic, which is clearer but cracks on impact. Not standard polycarbonate, which yellows in sunlight.
  • Floating mount: polycarbonate grows about 4 mm across this width between a cold winter night and a hot summer day. Clamp it between front and rear EPDM gaskets with about 5 mm free at every edge, and drill no holes in it.

4.5 Cooling and moisture

Air rises through the case by itself: in at the bottom, past the PSU and Pi, and out at the top under the hood (Figure 1).

Heat loadAirflow for a 10 °C riseApproach
80 W (normal content)14 CFMNatural convection through the vents below
150 W26 CFMNatural convection is marginal; add the fan
400 W (full white)70 CFMFan required. The software avoids this with mostly-black layouts and overnight dimming
  • Vent area: by the stack effect over a 0.5 m rise, 14 CFM needs about 30 in² (200 cm²) of net open area at both top and bottom. Insect mesh roughly halves the open area, so cut about 60 in² of slots in each face: a band of 5 × 60 mm slots, about 65 in each half-skin.
  • Rain: the bottom slots face down, so rain cannot enter them. The top slots sit under the hood, which overhangs the front and doubles as a drip edge over the window.
  • Mesh: stainless or aluminium insect screen bonded behind every slot band.
  • Fan (optional; fit it if testing shows a need): one 120 mm 5 V fan at the top exhaust, run from the 5 V supply through a thermostat switch set to about 40 °C. Alternatively, drive it from the Pi's fan header and let the Pi decide.
  • Condensation: the vents keep air moving, so no separate breather is needed. Slope the bottom skin slightly toward the slots so any water drains out.
  • The Pi still needs its own heatsink (see HARDWARE.md); case vents do not replace it.

4.6 Electrical

  • Earth the case. A metal case on mains power must be bonded to protective earth: a ground lug on the frame, a green/yellow lead to the PSU's earth terminal and the cord's earth conductor, and star washers or masked bare spots wherever powder-coated parts must connect.
  • Mains entry through a PG13.5 nylon cable gland (6 to 12 mm cord) in the bottom face, with a drip loop below it.
  • Fuse: keep the inline fuse and switch. Find the fuse rating in the PSU datasheet and label it; it is still marked unknown on the wiring diagram.
  • Outlet: a GFCI outlet with an extra-duty in-use cover, for example the Taymac MG500GS combo, installed by an electrician.
  • While the case is open, fix the Pi's under-voltage: feed it from the PSU terminals with a dedicated 16 AWG pair (HARDWARE.md §1, docs/wiring-diagram.pdf).

6. Build, test and install

6.1 Build order

  1. Measure the real panel assembly (backplates, panel depth, bracket positions), the PSU and the Pi stack. Weigh the current assembly.
  2. Draw the case in CAD from this plan, and produce flat patterns for the Figure 3 parts with slots, rivnut holes and gussets.
  3. Order the sheet metal, angle, window and hardware. Cut and drill the angle, and bolt up the frame square (check the diagonals).
  4. Set the rivnuts; fit the panel backplates and electronics tray; route ribbons and power; fit gaskets and skins.
  5. Fit the window between its gaskets and retainers, then the hood and the case Z-rail.

6.3 Maintenance

  • Two people lift the case off the rail after removing the two anti-lift screws, and open the back on a bench.
  • Network and quick checks go through the bottom hatch without taking the case down.
  • Every spring: clean the window with mild soap only (no ammonia or solvents on polycarbonate), clear the mesh, and check anchors and gaskets.

7. Open questions

  • SEPTA approval: who signs off, and do they require their own contractor or a stamped design?
  • Exact location: under the canopy or exposed; mounting height; distance to an outlet; is there a GFCI outlet already?
  • Wall: solid or cored brick; mortar condition; anchors in brick or in mortar joints (test hole)
  • Measured weight of the current assembly (replaces the 60 lb estimate)
  • Measured panel assembly depth and PSU dimensions (sets the 120 mm case depth)
  • Fuse rating from the PSU datasheet
  • Who installs the mains wiring and lifts the case: SEPTA facilities, Swarthmore facilities, or a contractor
